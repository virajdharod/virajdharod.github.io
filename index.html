About

Building quantum experiments end to end.

I am a final-year Ph.D. candidate in Physics at the University of California, Santa Barbara, with more than five years of experience in quantum hardware engineering and applied device physics.

My work spans experiment design, fiber and free-space optics, RF and electronic instrumentation, cryogenic integration, measurement automation, modeling, troubleshooting, and analysis. I am most at home where device physics meets the practical work of making a complex experiment reliable.

Experience

A trajectory through quantum optics, sensing, and experimental systems.

Oct 2020 — Present Santa Barbara, CA

Graduate Student Researcher

University of California, Santa Barbara · Advisor: Prof. Ania C. Bleszynski Jayich

  • Designed, built, and optimized fiber and free-space optical systems integrating lasers, photodetectors, AOMs, EOMs, RF electronics, and synchronized control hardware.
  • Characterized diamond optomechanical devices with mechanical quality factors above 10 million and developed models for performance-limiting mechanisms including optical-absorption-induced heating.
  • Built out and commissioned a dilution-refrigerator measurement platform with cryogenic optical, RF, DC, and positioning hardware for measurements below 250 mK.
  • Demonstrated quantum ground-state operation of GHz-frequency diamond optomechanical devices using direct thermometry.
  • Developed a modular Python control and data-acquisition framework for automated characterization, closed-loop control, synchronized measurements, and parameter extraction.
